# AI governance market brief (10 November 2026)

**Summary:** The market is still small, but demand is now coming mainly from companies that need to control AI agents in production. Large platform vendors and security vendors are moving in, mostly by buying smaller companies. The EU has pushed back its main high-risk deadlines, and US rules remain split by state.

Analyst view: Gartner Magic Quadrant\n\nGartner published its first Magic Quadrant for AI Governance Platforms in June 2026, covering 13 vendors. The placements below come from secondary write-ups, so check them against the Gartner document before quoting them.\n\n- **Leaders:** IBM (watsonx.governance), ServiceNow and Truyo.\n- **Visionaries:** Credo AI, OneTrust, Monitaur, Airia and ModelOp. One write-up leaves out ModelOp.\n- **Challenger:** Holistic AI.\n- **Niche Players:** Cranium AI, Relyance AI, Saidot and SAP.\n- **Named but not fully evaluated:** Enzai, LatticeFlow AI, Modulos, Singulr, Trustible and WitnessAI.\n\nHaving a Magic Quadrant at all matters: it means buyers now treat this as its own purchasing category, not as a feature of GRC (governance, risk and compliance), privacy or MLOps tools.\n\n## 3. Structural trends\n\n1. **The focus is moving from governing models to governing agents.** Buyers now ask which agents exist, what they can access, and what they did. Model risk documentation alone no longer covers it.\n   - Microsoft Agent 365 became generally available on 1 May 2026. It is a registry and control layer covering Microsoft-built, partner-built and custom agents.\n   - ServiceNow AI Control Tower was extended at Knowledge 2026. A preview integration with Agent 365 is available.\n   - Google's Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform includes an Agent Registry and Agent Gateway.\n2. **Governance, security and identity are merging.** Recent acquisitions combine policy management with runtime enforcement, red-teaming and identity or permission data.\n3. **Large platforms are absorbing the category.** Hyperscalers, ITSM vendors (IT service management, such as ServiceNow), data platforms and compliance-automation vendors areadding governance to existing contracts. Independent vendors have to compete on breadth, regulatory depth, or neutrality across vendors.\n\n## 4. If it holds, it fits a market where consolidation is beating new venture funding.\n\n## 5. Regulation\n\n**EU AI Act.** The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) took effect on 27 July 2026 and pushed back the high-risk deadlines:\n- Stand-alone high-risk systems (Annex III: employment, credit, biometrics, education and similar) now have until **2 December 2027**.\n- High-risk AI built into regulated products (Annex I) now has until **2 August 2028**.\n- Prohibited practices (Article 5) and transparency duties (Article 50) were not moved and already apply.\n- **Effect on the market:** fewer purchases driven by an immediate deadline in 2026. Buying is likely to pick up again through 2027.\n\n**United States.**\n- There is still no federal law that overrides state AI laws. Washington is pushing through executive action instead:\n  - aDOJ AI Litigation Task Force (December 2025);\n  - a White House framework (March 2026) asking Congress to override state laws that impose an \"undue burden.\"\n- **Colorado:** its original AI Act was put on hold after a lawsuit by xAI, which the DOJ joined. It was then repealed and replaced by SB 26-189, which takes effect on 1 January 2027 and regulates automated decision-making in \"consequential decisions.\"\n- **Other state laws in force:** California SB 53, AB 2013and SB 942; Texas TRAIGA; Illinois HB 3773.\n- **Effect on the market:** companies face a patchwork of state rules, which favours tools that map one set of controls across several frameworks.\n\n## 6. Implications for a services firm\n\n- **Demand drivers:** keeping track of how many agents a company runs, preparing for Annex III by December 2027, ISO/IEC 42001 certification (the AI management system standard), and mapping controls across several US states.\n- **Deliveryopportunity:** clients often run several tools at once, such as Microsoft Agent 365, ServiceNow AI Control Tower, OneTrust or IBM, and a cloud provider's native controls. Integrating these into one operating model is work a single vendor's product does not cover.\n- **Risk:** platform vendors bundling governance into existing licences may shrink spendingon stand-alone tools. Advisory and integration work is less exposed to this than reselling.\n\n## Caveats\n\n- The market-size figures come from commercial research firms with different definitions, so use them only as a rough guide.\n- The Gartner placements, some deal dates, and the Agent 365 price ($15 per user per month) come from secondary sources and are not confirmed.\n- My training data runs to June 2026.
